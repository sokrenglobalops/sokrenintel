What this is

SOKREN Global Operations is an open situational-awareness platform. The monitor plots tracked situations on a world map by intelligence priority, layers live signals over them, and pairs each situation with a structured analytic assessment: what is happening, why, and what is likely next. Anyone can use it. Nothing requires an account.

How to read it

  • Priority is the editorial tier of a situation: P1 Critical, P2 High, P3 Medium, P4 Low. Cyan-ringed markers are emerging flashpoints.
  • Layers add live context: military aircraft worldwide, all traffic within 250 nm of a selected situation, live AIS vessel positions around chokepoints and the selected situation, maritime chokepoints, and the world's submarine cable systems.
  • Live signals inside a situation are proximity counts from those layers. Co-location is a cue to look, not corroboration.
  • Search (⌘K / Ctrl+K) finds countries, situations, chokepoints, cables, and current headlines, and can run a live coverage search on any topic.

Methods

Each situation carries an Analysis of Competing Hypotheses with a stated discriminator, linchpin (key-assumption) analysis with explicit failure consequences, and a situation-adapted SWOT (stabilizing factors, vulnerabilities, de-escalation paths, escalation drivers). Probabilities are structured analyst judgments consistent with ICD 203 estimative language, not model outputs. Assessments are dated and revised as situations move.

Data sources

  • Coverage: collected daily from think tanks and conflict analysts (International Crisis Group, CFR, CSIS, Institute for the Study of War, Atlantic Council, RAND, AEI, Stimson Center, 38 North, War on the Rocks, Long War Journal, Bellingcat, Geopolitical Monitor, Lawfare, InSight Crime, Balkan Insight, Foreign Policy, The Diplomat); wire services and international news (AP, Reuters, CNN, The New York Times, The Washington Post, The Wall Street Journal, PBS NewsHour and Frontline, ABC News, CBS News, NBC News, NPR, Politico, Axios, BBC World, Al Jazeera, The Guardian, Financial Times, The Economist, France 24, DW, Kyiv Independent); official and humanitarian sources (UN News, ReliefWeb, Security Council Report, U.S. Department of Defense, U.S. State Department, CISA); cyber threat intelligence (Google Threat Intelligence / Mandiant, SentinelLabs, The Record, CyberScoop); and the GDELT Project for global coverage. Outlets without a public feed are reached through GDELT's index. Every item links to its original publisher.
  • Aircraft: OpenSky Network (one worldwide snapshot about every 2 minutes, shared through the SOKREN relay), with community ADS-B feeds (adsb.lol, adsb.fi) as fallback. Military identification is by ICAO hex allocation, so military aircraft without ADS-B, or outside known ranges, won't appear; absence on the map is not absence in the sky.
  • Ships: AISStream.io (live AIS, user-supplied free key). Cables: TeleGeography Submarine Cable Map (CC BY-NC-SA). Basemap: Natural Earth.

Limits

Situation briefs, priority tiers, and index scores are editorial and can lag fast-moving events. Live layers depend on public feeds and community relays and can be intermittent. Cable routes are TeleGeography's public map geometry, which is indicative rather than survey-accurate; when it cannot be reached, a small set of curated trunk corridors is shown instead. This is an open tool for awareness, not a substitute for professional intelligence support. Not affiliated with any government.
